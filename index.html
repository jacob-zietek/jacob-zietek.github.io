About me

I'm currently living in Houston and working on Embodied AI at Persona AI as one of the founding team members. I specifically work on creating robust manipulation policies for our multi-purpose humanoid, and contribute to any other deep learning-related work that might pop up :)

I was previously working on computer vision applications as one of the first ML hires at Armada AI, and have done internships with Amazon (healthcare NLP before ChatGPT), Shield AI (training fighter jet AI), PNNL (benchmarking sparse tensor algorithms), and BNL (HPC for covid research).

I graduated from Purdue CS in December 2023. My robotics undergraduate research was under Professor Ahmed Qureshi at CoRAL Lab where I worked on adding gaze control to visuolinguomotor policies and did some higher level planning work for a robot tour guide of Purdue.

Interests

AI and robots will eventually replace humans in boring and dangerous tasks. This is something we should welcome with open arms, and help accelerate as much as possible.

I’m broadly passionate about integrating embodied AI into our daily lives, and I enjoy working on frontier problems. Most of my work is deep-learning based.

I'm currently focused on developing robust robotic manipulation policies at Persona AI using robotic foundation models. My interests include integrating additional modalities (e.g., touch, sound) post-training, figuring out how to best incorporate visuomotor policies into larger robotic systems (e.g., selecting the right action representations and sensor placements), and improving robustness against real-world perturbations.

I'm also interested in high-level reasoning and collaborative decision-making between robots and humans. I like building things regular people can use.

My other technical interests are in space and defense. I have a gut feeling I'll end up working on space robots once space is commercialized more.

I love keeping up with the startup world & am interested in angel investing. Sometimes I'll give out microgrants for cool projects.
